NEP89 extension: 100 seeds

中文

5 additional systems, 31 algorithms, 100 seeds per cell (100–199). This ranking is separate from the published 45-system study; its scores are not directly comparable because the benchmark sets differ.

Score: 100 times the mean of clip(1 + log10(ERT_best / ERT) / 3, 0, 1). ERT counts steps to the first energy at or below E_ref + tolerance; timeouts without a hit are charged the full budget. Intervals use a normal approximation from the standard deviation of 1000 within-cell bootstrap samples.

E_ref is the lowest stored energy from the specified reference campaigns (or all campaigns when none are specified) for the current benchmark hash whose guard and wall energy is at most 0.01 eV. Guard-active runs are scored by energy, as in the original study; the counts below should be examined for artefacts. The reference is a putative NEP89 minimum, not a DFT global minimum.

GOFEE and SurrBH had a 3,600-second limit per seed. Time limits may affect hit rates and rankings, especially for costly systems.

System selection and experimental methods

The five systems are Cu4 and Mg4O4 variable-cell bulk crystals, Ni13, Ag4 on Cu(111), and Au4 on graphene. They are manually selected convenience samples, not a random representative set. The Cu and graphene hosts are ideal frozen structures with illustrative geometry; adsorption, substrate relaxation and alloying have not been independently validated. Initial sampling regions are not confinement constraints.

The 31 methods comprise 26 previously evaluated methods plus two experimental families and three mechanism controls, not five independent inventions. They combine established ideas; neither literature novelty nor general superiority has been established. Their earlier five-seed development comparisons were on previously used systems and are not independent evidence of generalization.

The six-system pilot used BH and Growth at seeds 30–34; all 60 saved best structures passed the baseline checks. Only five systems were selected for this study, whose seeds 100–199 are disjoint from the pilot. Ag4/MFI was held out because an Ag–Si contact and rigid-framework interface remain insufficiently validated; a prior Pd4/Al candidate was excluded after a disconnected saved structure. The full pilot audit retains the held-out MFI results. Earlier Mg4O4 trials also showed a short Mg–O contact and an initially short O–O distance; these are neither proven artefacts nor independently validated chemistry.

The additional candidate audit checked 15444 saved best structures within the reference window and found no failures of its energy, guard, distance, connectivity or applicable volume checks. Intermediate trace geometries were not saved; neither this audit nor zero guard energy proves all hits physical or identifies a global minimum. NEP89 is not DFT; the reported ionic-step score is not a wall-clock ranking and scores across different system sets cannot be compared directly.

Observed timeout and initial-region results

GOFEE had 499 timed-out runs out of 500; all 499 had already hit the energy window before the cap. The ionic-step ranking therefore retains those hits and does not measure the substantial wall-clock cost.

Among saved candidate structures in the reference window, at least one search atom lay outside the initial sampling box in 232/3110 Ni13, 1427/3059 Ag4/Cu(111), and 3055/3055 Au4/graphene structures. These counts include the selected pilot records. The box is an initialization region, not a confinement constraint, so this observation alone neither proves an artefact nor validates the interface chemistry.
